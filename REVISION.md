# Revision note

The line works because it opens with the worst thing first. No setup, no
softening, no joke to hide behind. The speaker says what happened in plain
words and then makes it worse in the very next sentence, which is what makes
the second half land: the disaster is not a single slip, it is a chain of
them, and the speaker knows it.

That order matters for this brief. The prompt asks for ownership before
cleverness, and the line follows that order. It states the damage, admits
the backup failure without blaming anyone else, and only then earns its
punch through understatement. The rhythm helps too: two short statements,
each complete on its own, the second one tightening the first.

It also sounds like something a person would actually say when caught, not
like a caption written for applause. The voice is flat, a little tired, and
that flatness is where the humour sits. Nothing in it reaches for a laugh;
the laugh comes from how calmly the catastrophe is reported.
